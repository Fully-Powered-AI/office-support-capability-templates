# Fully Powered Workflow Templates: Executive and Office Support

Meetings that end in decisions, and decisions that get done. These workflow templates take the preparation and follow-through around the working day and give an AI assistant a precise way to handle it, so nothing agreed in a room is lost on the way out.

This repository holds 12 workflow templates for executive assistants, office managers and anyone who runs a meeting. Each one is a single piece of work with its own folder: what it is for and who starts it, the complete template an AI assistant follows, a worked example with invented data, what to change for your company, what has been checked, and its sources.

## The workflow templates

- **[Board meeting pack](board-meeting-pack/)**: Before each board meeting, the CEO gets a pre-read built on the one decision the board is asked to make, with every figure from the company's own reports, an agenda kept mostly for discussion and the questions directors are likely to ask. Afterwards, a record of what was decided. The CEO sends it all.
- **[Business trip plan](business-trip-plan/)**: The assistant hands the traveller one plan for one business trip: dates and nights fixed from the brief, travel and hotel options with prices and the day each was checked, a day-by-day timetable around the meetings, the cost against travel policy and the bookings still to make. Nothing is booked or paid.
- **[Decision log](decision-log/)**: Your chief of staff keeps one register of what the leadership actually approved, across every meeting and message: each decision with who approved it, each action with one owner and a date, what is overdue, and a flag wherever a new decision clashes with an earlier one or revives a rejected idea.
- **[Delegation brief and follow-up](delegation-brief-and-follow-up/)**: Every task a manager hands over goes with a clear brief: what to deliver, in what form, by what date and time, why it matters, who will use it, what the person may decide alone and when the manager will check in. Each week, one list shows what is open, what is late and the next note to send.
- **[Executive week-ahead calendar review](executive-week-ahead-calendar-review/)**: Before the week starts, the executive assistant hands the executive one brief: every clash, every journey or run of meetings with too little time, every meeting with no stated purpose or no preparation time, and proposed time for the week's priorities. Nothing is accepted, declined, moved or sent.
- **[Inbox triage](inbox-triage/)**: The owner, or the assistant who keeps their inbox, starts the day knowing what needs a reply today, what can wait and until when, and what belongs to someone else, with a draft or a forwarding note for each. Nothing is sent, archived, deleted or moved, and no mailbox login is needed.
- **[Meeting agenda, notes and follow-ups](meeting-agenda-notes-and-follow-ups/)**: Whoever runs the meeting walks in with an agenda where every topic has an outcome and a time, and walks out with a one-page record: what was decided, who owns each action and by when, and what is still open. Nothing reaches anyone until that person has read it and sends it.
- **[Presentation outline and talk track](presentation-outline-and-talk-track/)**: Anyone presenting to a board, a client or a conference gets a storyboard built on one message, then a spoken script for every slide, timed to the slot and true to their own speaker notes. Every fact comes from what the presenter supplied, and the presenter approves the message and the words.
- **[Research summary brief](research-summary-brief/)**: An executive handed a pile of reports, articles or papers gets one brief: what each source found, where the sources agree and disagree, the limits of each, and exact quotations cited to the page. Built only from the documents supplied. What it means for the business stays the reader's call.
- **[Reviewer comments into one resolution list](reviewer-comments-resolution-list/)**: The owner of a document that came back from several reviewers gets one checklist: every proposed edit and comment, grouped by section, with clashes between reviewers set side by side. Every item stays open until the owner decides it, and the document itself is never touched.
- **[Shared folder filing plan](shared-folder-filing-plan/)**: The office coordinator gets a plan for tidying a shared folder: a proposed folder structure, every file's move with its owner named, and the duplicates and access questions to settle first. Owners approve their own moves; nothing is moved, renamed or deleted.
- **[Staff newsletter and FAQ roundup](staff-newsletter-and-faq-roundup/)**: Each month the office manager turns what teams send in into one staff newsletter, with answers to the questions people keep asking, each quoted from an approved source and its owner named. Nothing about one person's pay, health or performance goes in, and a person approves the issue before it goes.

## How to use one

Open a folder and start with its README. Every folder stands alone, so copy the one you want and leave the rest. The templates are written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini: paste the instruction block and the output format in as custom instructions, or load the folder in a product that reads `SKILL.md` packages.

To take the whole collection, press **Use this template** at the top of this page: it copies every folder into a repository in your own account, yours to change.

In every template a person starts the work, supplies the inputs and decides. The template prepares and checks. Anything that sends, posts, pays or changes a record waits for a person to approve it.

## What SKILL.md is

Each folder holds a file named `SKILL.md`. It is the technical file: the same instructions and output format as the template, in the packaged form that AI products which load instructions from a folder read. It is generated from `TEMPLATE.md`, so the two always agree. The workflow template is the whole folder; `SKILL.md` is one way of loading it. If your AI product does not read that format, use `TEMPLATE.md`.

## What "checked" means here

Each folder says exactly what was checked and when: who wrote it, that its sources and rights were reviewed, the date of its editorial review, and whether it has been tried on its invented example in a named AI product. Nothing is described as tested for a client unless it was. Test a template on your own past work before your team relies on it; each one comes with acceptance tests for that.

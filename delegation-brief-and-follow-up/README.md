# Delegation brief and follow-up

Every task a manager hands over goes with a clear brief: what to deliver, in what form, by what date and time, why it matters, who will use it, what the person may decide alone and when the manager will check in. Each week, one list shows what is open, what is late and the next note to send.

*Workflow template · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/delegation-brief-and-follow-up/)*

## The outcome

Work leaves the manager's desk once and lands clearly. The person who takes it on knows exactly what to hand back, when, for whom and how far they can go without asking. Nobody has to guess what "the survey thing" meant, and nobody finds out on the due date that the date never worked for them.

Then nothing goes quiet. Every Friday the manager and the person who tracks their commitments see each open task in one place: what is overdue and by how many working days, what was never acknowledged, what has been delivered and is waiting for the manager, and what falls due next week. Each task carries one next step, either a short neutral note drafted for the manager to send or a question only the manager can answer. The manager chooses what to delegate and to whom, approves every note and accepts every piece of work. The AI assistant sends nothing, changes nothing and says nothing about how well anyone works.

## Who starts the work, and when

The manager starts a brief: an owner, a managing director, a partner, a department head or any executive who hands work to the people around them. They run it at the moment of handing over, from a few lines typed in their own words.

The person who keeps the manager's list, usually an executive assistant or a chief of staff, runs the weekly list on a fixed day, takes the questions to the manager and sends the notes the manager approves.

## What they supply

- **For a brief:** the manager's note on the task, naming the one person it goes to.
- **For the weekly list:** the delegation list as a spreadsheet export, and the replies and updates that came in since last week, each with who sent it and when. The default column headers are set out in the template; if a header differs, the assistant stops and asks.
- **When available:** the approved briefs, so each check-in note asks for what that check-in was meant to show.
- **Once:** the working days and office closures, how soon a brief should be acknowledged, how check-ins are spaced, how late a task gets before the manager decides what happens, and how far ahead to look.

No mailbox, calendar, chat tool or task tracker is connected. Everything is pasted in by a person.

## What comes out

1. **A brief,** ready to hand over once the manager approves it: the deliverable, its format, what done means, the due date and time, why it matters, who will use it, what the person may do without asking, what is not part of the task, what to start from, and dated check-in points.
2. **Questions for the manager** wherever the note leaves a gap: no single person named, a loose date, no time, no reason, an internal name the person may not know.
3. **The weekly list** of open delegations, sorted into overdue, not yet acknowledged, delivered and waiting, due soon and on track.
4. **One next step per task:** a short follow-up note with the date to send it, or a question for the manager, such as whether to agree a new date or accept the work.
5. **The changes to make to the list,** each with the question it waits on.

A good brief can be read in a minute and acted on without a follow-up question. A good weekly list tells the manager in two minutes what needs them.

## What has to change for your company

Set your working days and office closures. Decide how soon a person should confirm they have a brief, how check-ins are spaced for short and long tasks, and how late a task gets before the manager decides what happens rather than another note going out. Choose who sends the notes: the manager, or the list keeper once the manager has approved them. Keep the default list columns or tell the assistant yours. Agree where the list lives, who may read it, and that it is never used to judge anyone's performance. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini; decide which notes and messages the assistant may be given.

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages.

# Executive week-ahead calendar review

Before the week starts, the executive assistant hands the executive one brief: every clash, every journey or run of meetings with too little time, every meeting with no stated purpose or no preparation time, and proposed time for the week's priorities. Nothing is accepted, declined, moved or sent.

*Capability template · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/executive-week-ahead-calendar-review/)*

## The outcome

The executive walks into Monday knowing what the week asks of them, and the problems are settled on Thursday instead of discovered at ten past nine. Two meetings booked on top of each other, a client visit that ends fifteen minutes before a stand-up across town, a four-hour morning with no gap, a "catch-up" nobody has explained, a board pack with no time to read it: each one is on the page, with the facts, as a question.

The priorities the executive named get real time, not hope. The brief finds the free hours that are actually usable, allows for the break and the journey on either side, and proposes blocks for each priority. Where the week cannot hold what was asked for, it says by how much, so the executive decides what gives instead of finding out on Friday.

The executive and the assistant choose every change. The capability never accepts, declines, moves or sends an invitation, and never puts anything in the calendar.

## Who starts the work, and when

The executive assistant, chief of staff or office manager who keeps the executive's calendar runs it once a week, a day or two before the week it covers, ahead of their weekly planning conversation with the executive. They run it again when the week shifts enough to matter. The executive reads the brief, makes the decisions, and the assistant makes the changes and asks the organisers the open questions.

## What they supply

- **A calendar export for the week,** with the date, times, title, place, organiser, attendees, the executive's answer and the purpose line for each event. The assistant makes the export; no calendar is connected.
- **The executive's priorities for the week,** in their order, with the hours each needs and whether mornings or afternoons suit it.
- **The preparation list:** the meetings that need reading or thinking time first, and how much.
- **Travel times** between the places the executive goes.
- **Working hours,** the base the executive works from, and any protected time, such as a school run.

## What comes out

One brief, always in the same order:

1. **The week at a glance:** for each day, how many meetings, how long they take and how much free time is left.
2. **Conflicts:** overlapping events, meetings over protected time, and anything outside working hours.
3. **Travel and breaks:** journeys with too little time, journeys nobody has timed, and long runs of meetings without a break.
4. **Purpose and preparation:** meetings with no stated purpose, and preparation with too little time set aside.
5. **Invitations not yet answered.**
6. **Proposed time reservations** for preparation and for each priority, with any shortfall.
7. **Decisions for the executive,** each with its facts and one question, and **questions for organisers,** for the assistant to ask.

Every figure in the brief can be added up again from the export, and nothing in it reads as a change already made.

## What has to change for your company

Set the working hours, the base and the protected times your executive actually keeps. Decide the shortest break worth keeping, the longest run of meetings before it is flagged, and how long a reserved block should be. Write down the travel times between the places your executive goes most; the capability never guesses a journey. Agree how preparation time is labelled in the calendar, which events are always shown as private, and whether the executive wants a view on conflicts or only the facts. It is written to run in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from an export the assistant makes; decide which calendar fields that export may include.

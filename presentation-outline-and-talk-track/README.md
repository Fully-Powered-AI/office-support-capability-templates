# Presentation outline and talk track

Anyone presenting to a board, a client or a conference gets a storyboard built on one message, then a spoken script for every slide, timed to the slot and true to their own speaker notes. Every fact comes from what the presenter supplied, and the presenter approves the message and the words.

*Capability template · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/presentation-outline-and-talk-track/)*

## The outcome

You stand up knowing exactly what you want the room to remember, and you can say it in one sentence. The talk is built on that sentence: a few points that make it true, the facts behind each, and a slide for each point whose headline says the point outright. Read the headlines alone and the story is already there.

Then you get the words. A script for each slide, in your voice, that fits the time you have at the pace you actually speak, with a table that shows where the minutes go. Where your speaker notes correct a slide, the script follows your notes and tells you what the screen still says. Nothing is added that you did not supply: every figure in the script is listed with where it came from, and every gap is a question for you, never a guess.

## Who starts the work, and when

The presenter starts it: an owner or managing director with a board slot, a partner or account lead with a client pitch, a founder in front of investors, anyone with a conference talk. An executive assistant or chief of staff can run it for them; the presenter still approves.

It runs twice. The first pass is early, while the talk is still notes and a goal. The second is once the slides are built, a few days before the day, leaving time to rehearse.

## What they supply

- **For the storyboard:** the occasion and date, who will be in the room and what they already know, the length of the slot, what the audience should decide or do afterwards, and the presenter's notes and figures.
- **For the script:** the approved storyboard and the slides as text, each with its headline, what else is on it and the speaker notes, plus who speaks before and after.
- **Once:** how fast the presenter speaks, how much of the slot to leave unscripted, and the most slides a slot should carry.

No drive, mailbox or presentation software is connected. Everything is pasted in by a person.

## What comes out

1. **A storyboard.** The one message, the supporting points with their facts, the shape of the talk, a headline and a plain description for every slide, the headlines read as one paragraph, and what was left out and why.
2. **A talk track.** A spoken script for each slide, with pauses and cues marked, a bridge into each slide and a handover at the end.
3. **A timing table.** Words and time per slide and in total, against the time available, with the lines to drop if the talk runs long.
4. **Three checks.** Every place your notes and your slides disagree, everything held back because it is not yet public, and every fact in the script with its source.

The slides themselves stay yours: the capability describes what each slide must show and never designs or edits one.

## What has to change for your company

Set each presenter's pace, ideally from one timed read-aloud. Decide how much of a slot stays unscripted and how many slides a slot may carry. Pick a default shape for your talks, or keep ours: where we stand, what it means, what we ask. Agree how unreleased figures are marked in notes, so the capability can hold them back. If your presentation software exports slides with different labels, set yours once. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini; decide which material the assistant may be given.

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.

# Presentation outline and talk track

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare one presentation with the person who will give it, in two passes. First a storyboard: the occasion, the one message, the points and facts that carry it, and a headline for every slide. Then, once the presenter has approved the storyboard and built the slides, a spoken script for each slide, sized to the slot at the presenter's pace, with a timing table. The presenter's speaker notes decide wherever they disagree with the slide. Every figure, name and date in the output comes from the presenter's material and is listed with its origin. The assistant designs no slides, adds no claims, changes no file and sends nothing.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it for a talk with a named presenter and a fixed slot: a board or investor update, a client presentation or pitch, a conference or panel talk, an all-staff briefing. Run the first pass when the talk is still an idea and a pile of notes; run the second once the slides exist, a few days before, so there is time to rehearse.

Do not use it for:

- slide design: layouts, charts, colours, images or animation;
- a written paper or pre-read that people read before a meeting instead of hearing it;
- turning a recorded talk into an article afterwards, which is a separate capability;
- a talk whose facts the presenter cannot supply, such as market figures nobody has yet;
- words to be delivered as if they were someone else's, without that person approving them.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the presenter, or an executive assistant, chief of staff or colleague preparing it for them.
- **Decides:** the presenter. They approve the storyboard, answer every open question, build and own the slides, and approve the final words.
- **Supplies facts:** whoever the presenter names for a figure they do not have to hand. The assistant asks the presenter, never that person.
- **The AI assistant:** drafts the storyboard and the script, counts and times them, and lists every gap. It contacts nobody.

## Inputs

Everything is pasted or attached by a person. No mailbox, drive or presentation software is connected.

First pass, the storyboard:

1. **The brief:** today's date; the presenter's name and role; the occasion and its date; who is in the audience and what they already know; the slot in minutes, and whether questions come after it outside the slot; what the audience should decide, do or think afterwards; a working title; and the shape the presenter wants, if any. If the slot or the goal is missing, ask and stop.
2. **The presenter's material:** notes, figures, earlier papers, anything the talk may draw on, in any form. If there is none, stop: there is nothing to build a message from.
3. **How they speak:** steady, ordinary or quick, or a words-a-minute figure from a rehearsal. If not given, use ordinary.

Second pass, the talk track:

4. **The approved storyboard,** with the presenter's answers to its questions. If it was never approved, stop and ask for approval first.
5. **The slides,** one block per slide, each line starting with one of four labels, exactly: "Slide:" with the number; "Headline:" with the headline as it appears on the slide; "On the slide:" with all other text on the slide, as written; and "Speaker notes:" with the presenter's notes for that slide, or "none". A table or export with the columns `Slide`, `Headline`, `On the slide` and `Speaker notes` is read the same way. If a label or column heading differs from these, or a slide is missing one, stop and ask which text is which; never guess. The one exception is slides supplied as pictures instead of text. Pictures carry no speaker notes, so read the headline and the other text off each picture, set that slide's notes to "none", and say under "Where the script follows your notes" that the slides came as pictures. If any words on a picture cannot be read for certain, ask for them as text instead of guessing.
6. **Who comes before and after,** if anyone: a chair who introduces the presenter, a next speaker, or questions.
7. **Your settings:** [your pace values] (default: steady 120, ordinary 140, quick 160 words a minute); [your unscripted allowance] (default: 10 per cent of the slot, left for pauses, clicks and the unexpected); [your slide limit] (default: one slide for every 30 seconds of the slot, rounded down); [your default shape] (default: where we stand, what it means, what I ask or propose).

## The procedure

First pass, the storyboard:

1. **Check the brief.** Confirm the occasion, audience, slot, goal and pace. Work out the scripted target: the slot minus [your unscripted allowance], in minutes and seconds, multiplied by the pace, in whole words.
2. **Write the one message.** One sentence that tells this audience what the talk means for them and what is asked of them, from the presenter's own material. Where the goal is a decision or an action, the message names it.
3. **Find the supporting points.** Two to four points that make the message true, each with the facts from the material that back it, quoted as given. A point with no fact behind it is written as such and becomes a question; the assistant never supplies a figure, example or source. A term the material uses without saying what it covers (a group of sites, customers or products named but never listed or counted) becomes a question too: the storyboard uses the reading the material most plainly supports and says which reading it took.
4. **Choose the shape.** Use the presenter's shape if given, otherwise [your default shape]. Lead with the message: the first slide says where the talk is heading.
5. **Draft the slides.** One point to a slide. Each slide gets a headline that states its point as a full sentence, using the presenter's figures where they have them; a line saying which point it carries, or Opening or Ask; and a line saying in plain words what the slide must show. Stay within [your slide limit]. If the material needs more slides than that, say how many and which points could merge.
6. **Read the headlines in order.** Run them together as one paragraph. If they do not tell the story without the slides, fix the headlines before returning.
7. **List what was left out.** Every item of the material the storyboard does not use, with one line on why.
8. **List what to settle.** Each gap, unclear fact or choice the presenter must make, as one question. Return the storyboard to the presenter, who approves it, changes it or answers the questions. The presenter builds the slides.

Second pass, the talk track:

9. **Read every slide's headline, text and notes.** Check the slides against the approved storyboard. A slide added, dropped or with a changed headline is used as the presenter built it and noted under what to settle. A storyboard question the presenter approved without answering stays open: carry it into what to settle as it stands, and where the script needs its answer, put a [CHECK] in its place.
10. **Compare notes and slide.** Where the speaker notes and the headline or slide text give a figure, name, date or claim that does not match, the notes govern the script. The same holds where a slide's headline and its other text disagree with each other: the notes decide if they speak to it; if they do not, use neither, put a [CHECK] and ask. List each difference: what the slide says, what the notes say, what the script says, and that the audience will see the slide's version on screen until the presenter changes it. The assistant changes no slide.
11. **Hold back what is not public.** Anything in the notes or slides marked confidential, internal, not public or not yet agreed, or that the notes say to be careful with, stays out of the script and becomes a question for the presenter. Names the notes say not to use stay out. A line such as "don't name them" covers the people or businesses it refers to; a name the notes or the presenter's answers give with no such limit may be used. Where it is unclear whom the line covers, leave the name out and ask.
12. **Write the script, slide by slide,** in the presenter's voice, as they would say it aloud: I and we, whole sentences that are easy to say in one breath, the presenter's own contractions (I'll, we've) where they would use them, never clipped slide points. Begin every slide with a line that carries the listener over from the one before. Make the slide's headline point in words. Where the notes say how to run a slide (open with the ask, keep it short), do what they say first; the headline's point is still made on that slide. Use only facts from the slide, its notes, the approved storyboard and the presenter's material. Write [CHECK: what is needed, and from whom if the notes say] only where the notes or the storyboard say a figure is still to come for that slide, or where a sentence the slide needs cannot be said without a missing fact; add a question for each. A gap the script can do without becomes a question with no [CHECK]. Close the last slide by handing over to whoever comes next, as given in the inputs.
13. **Mark delivery cues** in square brackets: [pause] where a point needs to land, and [point to ...] or [next slide] where the presenter acts. Use them sparingly.
14. **Count and time.** Write figures in the script the way the presenter will say them where the spoken form is longer ("three hours ten minutes", not "3h10"), so the count follows the speech. Count the words of each slide's script exactly as written, leaving out everything in square brackets; a word is anything between spaces, so a figure or a hyphenated word counts once. Time each slide and the total as words divided by the pace, rounded to the nearest second; time the total from the total words, so slide times may differ from it by a second or two. Compare the total with the scripted target and the slot.
15. **Stay within the target.** If the total is over the scripted target, shorten bridges and repetition first. If covering everything in the notes still runs over, keep the notes' content, say by how much, and list the cuts that would bring it within the target; the presenter decides. If the total is under the target, say by how much and add nothing: the spare time joins the allowance.
16. **Name what to cut on the day.** Up to two passages the presenter could drop if they run long, each with its words and seconds. A cut may remove a bridge, a repeat or a supporting detail; it never removes the one message, the ask, or a figure a headline depends on.
17. **List the facts used.** Every figure, name and date in the script, each with where it came from: the slide text, the notes, the storyboard material or the brief. A figure the assistant worked out, such as "more than twice as long", shows its working.
18. **Return everything to the presenter.** When they answer a question, update the script, recount and retime, and note the answer as "supplied by [name]".

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No slot, no goal or no material:** ask and stop.
- **Asked to write the second pass before the storyboard is approved:** say so, and offer the first pass.
- **Slide labels or column headings do not match:** stop and ask which text is which. Slides supplied as pictures are read as set out under Inputs, with no notes; words that cannot be read for certain are asked for as text.
- **The notes and the slide disagree:** the notes govern the script; list the difference with the on-screen warning.
- **The notes contradict each other, or contradict the approved storyboard:** quote both, use neither in the script, put a [CHECK] in its place and ask.
- **Material marked confidential or not public:** keep it out and ask.
- **Asked to add a figure, result, quotation, customer name or example the material does not contain, or to make a claim sound stronger than the material supports:** decline, and ask the presenter for the source.
- **Asked to design slides, choose charts or edit the deck file:** decline; describe in words what each slide must show, and leave the slides to the presenter.
- **Asked to send the script, put it into the deck, or share it with anyone:** decline and return the text for the presenter to use.
- **A slide or note touches legal, medical, financial or employment advice:** keep the presenter's own wording, add nothing to it, and list it under what to settle so the presenter can check it with whoever owns that subject.

## Reviewer checklist

The presenter checks before rehearsing:

1. The one message is what they want the audience to leave with, in words they would say.
2. Every figure, name and date in the script appears under Facts used, and each matches its origin.
3. Nothing in the script comes from outside the material: no new figure, result, quotation or example.
4. Every difference between notes and slide is listed, and the slides have been corrected where the screen should match.
5. Nothing marked confidential or not public is in the script.
6. Every [CHECK] has been answered and the script retimed.
7. The total is within the scripted target, and a read-aloud rehearsal lands inside the slot.
8. The last slide hands over to the right person.

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Storyboard from notes.** A 3-minute board slot, ordinary pace, six facts and one goal. Expected: scripted target 378 words (2:42), one message naming the ask, supporting points each with their facts, headlines that read as a story, slide limit 6 respected.
2. **Point with no fact.** A supporting point the material gives no figure for. Expected: written as "no fact supplied" with a question. Fails if a figure appears.
3. **Unused material.** A fact that does not support the message. Expected: listed under Left out with a reason.
4. **Second pass without approval.** Slides supplied, storyboard never approved. Expected: asks for approval and offers the first pass.
5. **Labels that do not match.** Slides pasted with "Title" and "Body" in place of "Headline" and "On the slide". Expected: stops and asks which text is which.
6. **Notes and slide disagree.** The slide says 40 sites, the notes say 41. Expected: script says 41; difference listed with the on-screen warning; slide unchanged.
7. **Missing figure.** Notes say a number is coming from a colleague. Expected: [CHECK] in the script, a question under To settle, and the placeholder not counted in the words.
8. **Not public.** Notes mention talks with a landlord that are not public yet. Expected: left out of the script and raised as a question.
9. **Under target.** The notes support only 321 words against a 378-word target. Expected: says under by 57 words (0:24) and adds nothing.
10. **Over target.** Dense notes produce 450 words against 378. Expected: keeps the notes' content, states the overrun, lists cuts; drops nothing on its own.
11. **Asked for a stronger claim.** "Say we're the best in the region." Expected: declines unless the material supports it, and asks for the source.
12. **Asked to edit the deck.** "Put this into my speaker notes in the file." Expected: declines and returns the text.
13. **Slides as pictures.** Five slide pictures, no text and no notes, one with a figure too blurred to read. Expected: headline and text read off the four clear pictures, notes set to "none", the pictures line under "Where the script follows your notes", and the blurred figure asked for as text, not guessed.
14. **Headline and text disagree, notes silent.** The headline says 12 weeks, the slide text says 10, the notes do not mention it. Expected: neither figure in the script, a [CHECK] in its place, the difference listed and asked about.

## What to customize

- [your pace values], and whether each presenter records their own pace from a timed rehearsal.
- [your unscripted allowance], larger for a talk with live demonstrations or audience questions inside the slot.
- [your slide limit] and [your default shape].
- Your house words for slide labels, if your deck exports use different ones; change the labels in the inputs and the instructions together.
- Which occasions use it first: board updates, client pitches or conference talks.
- Who may run it for a presenter, and how the presenter's approval of the storyboard is recorded.
- Which material the assistant may be given in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and your rule on unreleased figures.

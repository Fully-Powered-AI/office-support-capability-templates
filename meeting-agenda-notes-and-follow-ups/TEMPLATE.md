# Meeting agenda, notes and follow-ups

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare one meeting's paperwork for the person who runs it. Before the meeting: a draft agenda with an outcome, a lead and a time for every topic. After it: a one-page record of decisions, actions with one owner and a date each, parked items and open questions, with a follow-up draft. Every gap in the notes becomes a question for that person. The assistant never chooses an owner or a date, and never sends, invites or changes a record.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it for a meeting with a named person running it: a weekly team or operations meeting, a project meeting, a leadership meeting, a committee meeting. Run the first part a day or two before, and the second part the same day the meeting ends.

Do not use it for:

- board, shareholder or other minutes that must follow a set form and be formally adopted;
- a disciplinary, grievance or performance meeting about an individual;
- a word-for-word account of who said what, because the output is decisions and owners;
- a meeting nobody runs, until someone is named to run it.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the person who runs the meeting, or an assistant or coordinator working for them.
- **Decides:** the person who runs the meeting. They settle every gap with the people concerned, approve the agenda and the record, and send both.
- **Owners:** the people named on each action. They confirm their own action and date when the follow-up arrives.
- **The AI assistant:** prepares drafts and lists gaps. It contacts nobody.

## Inputs

Everything is pasted or attached by a person. No calendar, mailbox or meeting tool is connected.

Before the meeting:

1. **The basics:** meeting name, date, start time, length, who runs it, who is invited, and today's date. If the length is missing, ask; the times cannot be checked without it.
2. **The topics,** each with what the meeting should settle, who leads it and how many minutes it needs. A topic missing any of these is listed as a question, never completed by guesswork.
3. **Reading** people should do beforehand, if any.
4. **Open actions from last time,** with owner and due date. If none are supplied, the agenda says so.

After the meeting:

5. **The notes,** in any shape, from one note-taker or several, with today's date. Say whose they are. If there are no notes, stop.
6. **The agenda that was used** and the open actions from last time. If missing, the record says they were not checked.
7. **Your settings:** [your reading lead time] (default: one working day), [your read-back minutes] (default: 5), [your follow-up recipients] (default: everyone invited, absent people included), and [your held-back subjects] (default: an individual's pay, performance, health or conduct).

## The procedure

Before the meeting:

1. **Check the basics.** Confirm name, date, start, length, who runs it and who is invited.
2. **Give each topic an outcome.** Write it starting with Decide, Discuss or Inform, from the person's own words. Where they gave no outcome, ask what the meeting should settle; if the topic has a lead and minutes, it stays on the timed agenda as "Discuss: [outcome needed]" and is ordered with the Discuss topics. Each topic has one lead and a number of minutes; a topic with no lead or no minutes is left off the timed agenda and listed as a question.
3. **Ask whether the meeting is needed.** If no topic is a Decide or a Discuss, say that this could go out as a written update. The person chooses.
4. **Order the topics:** Decide first, then Discuss, then Inform, keeping the person's order within each group.
5. **Check the time.** Topic minutes plus the read-back must fit the length. If they do not, state the total, the length and the minutes over, and propose the smallest change, starting with taking an Inform topic off the agenda and sending it out in writing. Show the timed agenda with the proposal applied, list the topic taken off under the reading, and say in the "To settle" question that the agenda shows the proposal and that the person can choose a different change. Never shorten a Decide topic unasked. Show spare minutes as a line marked Spare.
6. **List the reading** with who supplies it and the date it goes out, counted back from the meeting by the reading lead time. Only reading the person supplied, or a topic the time proposal turned into reading, is listed. If there is none, write "None supplied".
7. **List open actions from last time** with owner and due date. Mark "due date passed" where the date is before today.
8. **Add the read-back and the note-taking key.** The read-back is the last slot: the person who runs the meeting says each decision and each action back to the room, naming who owns it and when it is due. The key: D decision, A action with its owner and due date, P parked, Q question, F fact.
9. **Return the draft agenda** with the "To settle" list first. The person sends it.

After the meeting:

10. **Read all the notes.** Where two note-takers disagree, quote both and list it under "To settle".
11. **Decisions.** Enter one only where the notes show something was settled. State what was decided, any condition, and who decided if the notes say, for each decision separately. "Talked about", "leaning towards" and "no objections so far" are not decisions; they become a parked item or an open question. A loose point for looking at a decision again ("revisit after January") stays in the decision as a condition, in the notes' words, and is not a gap.
12. **Actions.** Start each with a verb and name what will exist when it is done. Each has one owner and one date.
    - No owner in the notes ("someone", "we", "the team"): write OWNER NEEDED.
    - Two or more names: write ONE OWNER NEEDED and the names.
    - A stated day ("by Friday"): turn it into a date from the meeting date and keep the words in brackets.
    - No date, or a loose one ("soon", "this week", "ASAP"): write DATE NEEDED and keep the words.
13. **Parked items.** What it is, why it was not settled, and what brings it back: a date or an event. If the notes give neither, write RETURN POINT NEEDED.
14. **Open questions.** Each with who finds the answer and by when, or NEEDED. Enter each thing once: something a named person will do or find out is an action, and is not entered again as an open question.
15. **Open actions from last time.** Mark each Done, Still open or Not known, from the notes only, with "due date passed" where it applies.
16. **For the record.** Facts and figures as noted, numbers copied exactly. Where a figure differs from the agenda or the reading, show both. A fact already shown under Decisions or under last time's actions is not repeated here.
17. **Leave out and hold back.** Leave out who argued what, how anyone felt and side remarks. Anything on a held-back subject, the reason for someone's absence included, goes under "Held back", for the person who ran the meeting only, and never into the follow-up draft. That person removes the "Held back" line before the record is filed or shared.
18. **Next meeting.** Give a date only if the notes state one. Propose topics from the parked items, open questions and open actions.
19. **Count the gaps** and list them first, each as one question. One item is one gap, even when it lacks both an owner and a date. An action from last time marked Not known is a gap: a question for its owner. Where the agenda set an outcome for a topic and the notes do not show it was reached, say so in the question, parked item or open question for that topic.
20. **Write the follow-up draft:** one message to the follow-up recipients with decisions, actions by owner, what was not decided and what is open, and nothing the output format does not have. The person who ran the meeting sends it, so they are not among its recipients. While any gap is open, head it "NOT READY TO SEND" with the count and show each gap in square brackets.
21. **Return everything to the person who ran the meeting.** When they give answers, update the record and the draft, note each answer as "set by [name] after the meeting", and recount the gaps.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **The notes hold no decisions and no actions:** say so in one line and return the facts only. Invent nothing.
- **A recording transcript is supplied in place of notes:** work from it the same way, and still return decisions and owners, never a summary of who said what.
- **A decision needs someone who was not in the room,** such as spending above a limit: record it with that condition, exactly as noted.
- **A held-back subject appears:** keep it out of the record's circulated parts and the draft; list it under "Held back".
- **Told to pick an owner or a date itself:** decline and ask the person to name one. A name or date the person gives is theirs to give and is recorded as set after the meeting.
- **Asked to send the follow-up, issue the invitation, or update a calendar, task list or tracker:** decline. Return the text for the person to send or enter.
- **Asked to add something the notes do not show:** add it only as "added by [name] after the meeting".

## Reviewer checklist

The person who ran the meeting checks, before anything goes out:

1. Every topic on the agenda has an outcome, a lead and a time, and the times add up to the meeting's length or less.
2. Every decision in the record is something the room settled, worded the way the room would recognize.
3. Every action has exactly one owner and one date, and no owner or date was supplied by the assistant.
4. Every parked item says what brings it back.
5. Every figure matches the notes.
6. Nothing on a held-back subject appears in the follow-up draft.
7. The "To settle" count is zero and the draft is no longer headed "NOT READY TO SEND".
8. Last time's open actions are all accounted for.

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Agenda that fits.** Three topics of 15, 10 and 10 minutes in a 45-minute meeting. Expected: Decide topics first, a 5-minute read-back as the last slot, 5 minutes shown as spare, "None supplied" under the reading, nothing to settle.
2. **Agenda that does not fit.** Topics totalling 45 minutes in a 45-minute meeting. Expected: states 50 against 45 and 5 over; proposes taking an Inform topic off the agenda and sending it out in writing; shows the timed agenda with that proposal applied and says so; shortens no Decide topic.
3. **Topic with no lead or time.** "New scheduling software", with no outcome, lead or time. Expected: left off the timed agenda and listed as a question. Fails if an outcome is invented.
4. **Nothing to decide.** Every topic is an update. Expected: says this could be a written update and leaves the choice to the person.
5. **Action with no owner.** Notes say "someone needs to get a lease quote". Expected: OWNER NEEDED and DATE NEEDED, and a question under "To settle". Fails if a name appears.
6. **Two owners and a loose date.** "Tom + Marcus to sort out a process" and "Tom to call them this week". Expected: ONE OWNER NEEDED with both names; DATE NEEDED with the words kept.
7. **Talked about, not decided.** Notes say "not deciding today, need a replacement price". Expected: a parked item with what brings it back, and no decision.
8. **Held-back subject.** Notes include a remark on one person's performance, and say another was out sick. Expected: the remark and the reason for the absence under "Held back" only, and neither in the rest of the record or in the follow-up draft. The absence itself is still recorded.
9. **Asked to send.** "Email this to the team." Expected: declines and returns the draft for the person to send.
10. **Empty notes.** Notes with facts and no decisions or actions. Expected: says none were found; invents none.
11. **Timed topic with no outcome.** "Parts returns", with a lead and 10 minutes and nothing said about what to settle. Expected: stays on the timed agenda as "Discuss: [outcome needed]", ordered with the Discuss topics, with a question under "To settle". Fails if an outcome is invented.

## Instructions for the AI assistant

Paste this block as custom instructions, followed by the output format above. The block tells the assistant to use that format and does not repeat it.

```
You prepare one meeting's agenda, record and follow-up draft for the person
who runs the meeting. You prepare; that person decides and sends. You never
send a message, issue or change an invitation, or update a calendar, task
list or any other record. You never choose an owner or a date.

Work only from what the person pastes or attaches. Never ask for a password,
a login code or access to a calendar or mailbox.

If the person supplies topics, do PART A. If they supply notes, do PART B.
Use the output format exactly. Put the "To settle" list first, one question
per gap, with a count. One item is one gap, even when it lacks both an owner
and a date. Keep the person's own name for the meeting. Name the person who
runs the meeting under "Run by" only: not again under Invited, Present or
To, because they are the one who sends.

PART A: DRAFT AGENDA
1. Confirm meeting name, date, start, length, who runs it, who is invited
   and today's date. If the length is missing, ask and stop.
2. Write each topic's outcome starting with Decide, Discuss or Inform, from
   the person's words. If a topic has a lead and minutes but no outcome,
   keep it on the timed agenda as "Discuss: [outcome needed]", order it with
   the Discuss topics and ask what the meeting should settle. If it has no
   lead or no minutes, leave it off the timed agenda and list it as a
   question. Never invent an outcome, a lead or a time.
3. If no topic is a Decide or a Discuss, say this could be a written update
   and let the person choose.
4. Order: Decide, then Discuss, then Inform, keeping the person's order
   within each group.
5. Add a read-back as the last slot ([your read-back minutes], default 5).
   Topic minutes plus the read-back must fit the length. If not, state the
   total, the length and the minutes over, and propose the smallest change,
   starting with taking an Inform topic off the agenda and sending it out in
   writing. Show the timed agenda with the proposal applied, list the topic
   taken off under the reading, and say in the "To settle" question that the
   agenda shows the proposal and the person can choose a different change.
   Never shorten a Decide topic unasked. Show minutes left over as a line
   marked Spare, before the read-back.
6. List the reading with who supplies it and the date it goes out
   ([your reading lead time], default one working day before). List only
   reading the person supplied, or a topic your proposal in step 5 turned
   into reading. If there is none, write "None supplied."
7. List open actions from last time with owner and due date; mark "due date
   passed" where the date is before today.
8. Add the note-taking key and the closing status line.

PART B: MEETING RECORD AND FOLLOW-UP DRAFT
1. Read all the notes. Take today's date from the person and never assume
   one. If two note-takers disagree, quote both and list it under "To
   settle". If a transcript is supplied in place of notes, work from it the
   same way: decisions and owners, never who said what. If there are no
   decisions and no actions, say so and return the facts only.
2. Decisions: only what the notes show was settled, with any condition. A
   decision that needs someone who was not in the room is recorded with
   that condition, as noted. A loose point for looking at a decision again
   ("revisit after January") stays in it as a condition, in the notes'
   words, and is not a gap. Give "Decided by" for each decision: who, if
   stated, or "not stated in the notes". Talked about is not decided: make
   it a parked item or an open question.
3. Actions: a verb and what will exist when done; one owner; one date.
   No owner: OWNER NEEDED. Several names: ONE OWNER NEEDED and the names.
   A stated day: convert to a date from the meeting date and keep the words
   in brackets. No date or a loose one (soon, this week, ASAP): DATE NEEDED,
   keeping the words.
4. Parked items: why not settled and what brings each back, or RETURN POINT
   NEEDED. Open questions: who finds out and by when, or NEEDED. Enter each
   thing once: what a named person will do or find out is an action, and is
   not entered again as an open question.
5. Open actions from last time: Done, Still open or Not known, from the
   notes only. Add "due date passed" to one that is not done and whose date
   is before today. Not known is a gap: a question for the action's owner.
6. For the record: facts and figures copied exactly. If a figure differs
   from the agenda or the reading, show both. Do not repeat a fact already
   shown under Decisions or Open from last time. If the agenda set an
   outcome for a topic and the notes do not show it was reached, say so in
   the question, parked item or open question for that topic.
7. Leave out who argued what, how anyone felt and side remarks. Put anything
   about [your held-back subjects] (default: an individual's pay,

   it out of the rest of the record and out of the follow-up draft.
8. Next meeting: a date only if the notes state one; propose topics from
   parked items, open questions and open actions.
9. Follow-up draft to [your follow-up recipients] (default: everyone

   READY TO SEND: n gaps" and show each gap in square brackets, a question

10. End with: "Draft for [name]. Nothing has been sent and no record has
```

## What to customize

- Which meetings use it, and who runs each.
- [your reading lead time] and [your read-back minutes].
- [your follow-up recipients]: everyone invited, or attendees and a wider list.
- [your held-back subjects], and who may see the "Held back" section.
- Whether actions carry a running number from meeting to meeting, and where the record is kept so the next agenda can pick up open actions.
- Your house style for dates, names and roles.
- Which notes and documents the assistant may be given in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and your rule on recording meetings.

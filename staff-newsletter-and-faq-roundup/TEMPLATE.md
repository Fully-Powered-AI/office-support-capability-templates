# Staff newsletter and FAQ roundup

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare one issue of a monthly staff newsletter from the updates teams send in, with a roundup of the questions staff keep asking, and an issue check for the person who approves it. Each news item names the team it came from and keeps its facts exactly as submitted. Each answer is quoted word for word from an approved source, with the source's owner and approval date beside it. Anything about one person's pay, health or performance stays out, people news goes in only with the person's agreement, and a change that has not yet been announced is sent back to its owner. The assistant prepares; a named person approves the issue and someone sends it. The assistant never sends, posts or schedules anything, and never writes an answer of its own.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once a month, a few days before the issue goes out, when the updates from teams and the list of questions staff have been asking are in. It suits a company of roughly 30 to 500 people with one newsletter that goes to everyone.

Do not use it for:

- announcing one decided change, such as a new policy, a restructure or a move to new software. Announce the change first, with its own messages; internal change announcement prepares those. Once announced, the newsletter can point to it;
- answering one person's question about their own leave, pay or situation. That goes to HR, or to policy answers from the handbook;
- anything about redundancies, a dispute, an investigation or an individual's employment;
- newsletters for customers, members or the public. This one is for staff only.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the office manager, chief of staff, executive assistant or whoever puts the newsletter together.
- **Approves the issue:** one named person, such as the managing director, the head of operations or the HR manager. They settle every item under "To settle" and approve the issue before it goes.
- **Sends it:** the person who runs it, once the approver has said yes, from their own email or chat account.
- **Submitters:** the people who put updates in the table for their team. They answer questions about their rows and record consent for people news.
- **Source owners:** the people who own each approved source. They confirm an old source still holds and write the answers the sources do not yet give.
- **The AI assistant:** prepares the draft issue and the issue check. It contacts nobody.

## Inputs

Everything is pasted or attached by a person. No mailbox, chat tool, intranet or HR system is connected.

1. **The issue settings.** Newsletter name and the issue month; today's date; the send date; the approver (name and role); who sends it; who readers contact with questions about the issue; and the settings below. If the issue month, the send date or the approver is missing, stop and ask.
   - [your wins window]: default, wins and people news dated in the issue month.
   - [your look-ahead]: default, coming-up items dated from the send date to six weeks after it.
   - [your item limit]: default 15 news items.
   - [your FAQ limit] and [your asked threshold]: default up to 6 questions, each asked at least 3 times.
   - [your quote limit]: default 60 words per answer.
   - [your source age limit]: default 12 months before the send date.
   - [your held-out subjects]: default, anything about one identifiable person's pay, bonus or benefits; health, injury, medical leave or the reason for an absence; performance, ratings, rankings, discipline or conduct.
2. **The team updates,** exported from the form or shared sheet teams fill in, as a table with these column headers: `team`, `submitted_by`, `type`, `item`, `date`, `link`, `consent`. `type` is one of win, coming up or people news. `date` is when the thing happened or will happen. `link` is optional. `consent` is "yes" when the person a people-news item is about has agreed to it being shared, and blank otherwise. Rows are numbered from 1 in the order supplied, not counting the header. If a header does not match, stop and ask which column holds what; never guess. If there are no rows, write "No team updates supplied" in the draft's news sections.
3. **The questions staff keep asking,** as a table with these column headers: `question`, `times_asked`, `where_asked`, `topic_owner`. `times_asked` is the person's own count of how many people asked. `topic_owner` is who should answer it if no source does. Questions are numbered from 1 in the order supplied. If a header does not match, stop and ask. If there is no table, the roundup reads "No questions supplied this month."
4. **The approved sources:** the documents answers may be quoted from, such as the staff handbook, a published notice or an announcement already sent. Each starts with its title, its owner and the date it was approved, followed by its text, with section numbers where it has them. A source with no owner or no approval date is not used, and the issue check says so.
5. **Last month's issue,** if available. If it is not supplied, the issue check says repeats were not checked.

If both the team updates and the questions are missing, stop: there is nothing to prepare.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm the settings, the column headers of both tables and the sources. Stop where the inputs section says to.
2. **Read each update row.** A row needs a team, a type and an item. If any is missing, or the type is not one of the three, list a question for the submitter under "To settle" and keep the row out of the draft until it is answered. A coming-up item with no date is treated the same way.
3. **Decide what stays out.** Check each complete row against these reasons, in this order, and give the first that applies:
   1. **Held-out subject:** the item touches [your held-out subjects]. It stays out even with consent, and the issue check names only the row, the team and the submitter, never what the item says. Praise of one person's results counts as performance.
   2. **No consent:** a people-news item without "yes" in the consent column. It comes in once the submitter records that the person agreed.
   3. **Not yet announced:** the item tells staff about a new or changed policy, benefit, pay arrangement, way of working they must follow, or team structure, and has no link to an announcement already made. It comes in once the change has been announced and the link is added.
   4. **Already in last issue:** the same news appeared last month and the row adds nothing new.
   5. **Outside this issue's dates:** a win or people news dated outside [your wins window], or a coming-up item dated outside [your look-ahead].
4. **Check the figures.** Copy every number exactly as submitted. If two rows give different figures for the same thing, list a question for both submitters under "To settle".
5. **Write each news item.** One or two sentences, with the facts and figures exactly as submitted and nothing added. Edit only for length, plain wording and tense to suit the send date. Wins and people news start with the team's name and keep the order supplied. Coming-up items go in date order, each starting with the weekday and the date from its `date` column and ending with the team in brackets. Dates inside an item's own words stay as submitted: add no weekday to them, and do not turn a range into the heading. Add the link as "More: [link]" where there is one.
6. **Check the item limit.** If the news items in the draft exceed [your item limit], keep them all in the draft, say under "To settle" how many are over, and name as candidates to cut the items that concern only the team that sent them. The approver chooses.
7. **Choose the questions.** Set aside any question about one person's own pay, leave or situation: it goes under "Questions not chosen" as one person's own case for HR, and the issue check does not repeat its wording. Set aside questions asked fewer than [your asked threshold] times. Rank the rest by times asked, keeping the order supplied on a tie, and take up to [your FAQ limit]; the rest are not chosen, as over the limit.
8. **Answer each chosen question from the sources.** Find the passage in one approved source that answers it. Quote, word for word, only the sentences that answer the question as asked, within [your quote limit]; leave out neighbouring sentences on related points the question did not ask about. If the answer itself runs past the limit, quote the part that answers the question and add "Read the rest in [source, section]". Under the quote, give the source's title, section, owner and approval date. Never paraphrase a source and never add words of your own to an answer.
   - **No source answers it:** it stays out of the draft and goes under "Answers needed", with the topic owner, or OWNER NEEDED if the table gives none.
   - **Two sources answer it differently:** it stays out of the draft and goes under "To settle", as a question for both owners.
   - **The source was approved before [your source age limit]:** the answer stays in the draft with "[owner's name to confirm this is still current]" after the quote, the source owner's name taking the place of "owner's name", and a question for the owner goes under "To settle".
9. **Write the opening line** of the issue from what the draft holds: how many wins, dates coming up, pieces of people news and answered questions, in the singular for one. Leave out any section that is empty.
10. **Mark the status.** While anything is under "To settle", the draft is headed "NOT READY TO SEND" with the count, as "1 item" or "n items". When nothing is, it is headed "Ready for [approver] to read and approve".
11. **Return the issue check and the draft** to the person who runs it. When answers come back, update both, mark each answer "settled by [name]" in the issue check, and recount. The approver approves; the person who runs it sends.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No team updates and no questions:** stop and say so.
- **A column header does not match the defaults:** stop and ask which column holds what.
- **No issue month, send date or approver:** stop and ask.
- **Asked to send, post or schedule the issue, or to email the submitters:** decline and return the text for the person to send.
- **Asked to write an answer that no source gives, or to soften or summarize a quote:** decline. List the question under "Answers needed"; the owner puts the answer in writing and approves it first.
- **Asked to include a held-out item because the person agreed:** decline and say why. The approver may change [your held-out subjects] in the settings; the assistant does not.
- **A row reads as news of job losses, a restructure, a pay change or a dispute:** keep it out as not yet announced, whatever link it has, and say in the issue check that it needs its own announcement, not a newsletter item.
- **A row or source contains a home address, a personal phone number, bank details or a password:** do not repeat it; tell the person to remove it from the source.
- **A question asks for a decision nobody has made** (such as "will we get a pay rise this year?"): no source answers it, so it goes under "Answers needed" for its owner.

## Reviewer checklist

The approver checks, before the issue goes:

1. Every news item traces to a row in the team updates, with its figures unchanged and its team named.
2. Nothing in the draft touches an individual's pay, health or performance, consent or not.
3. Every piece of people news has "yes" in the consent column.
4. No item announces a change for the first time; every change mentioned links to an announcement already made.
5. Every answer is a word-for-word quote from the source named, and the source's owner and date are right.
6. Every old source flagged has been confirmed by its owner, or the answer has been taken out.
7. "Answers needed" has gone to each owner, so next month's issue can answer them.
8. The "To settle" count is zero and the draft is headed "Ready" before anyone sends it.

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run on its own. The invented example's rows and questions exercise tests 3 to 10, test 2 in part (its health item has no consent recorded) and the ordinary items of test 1; tests 11, 12 and 13, and the linked half of test 5, have not been run.

1. **Ordinary month.** Five wins, three coming-up items and one consented welcome, all in their dates. Expected: all in the draft, teams named, coming-up items in date order with weekdays, nothing to settle.
2. **Health item with consent.** A people-news row saying someone is off after an operation, consent "yes". Expected: left out as a held-out subject; the issue check names the row, team and submitter only, not the content.
3. **Praise of one person's results.** A win naming the month's top performer. Expected: left out as a held-out subject.
4. **People news without consent.** A colleague's half marathon, consent blank. Expected: left out, with "comes in once the submitter records that the person agreed".
5. **Unannounced change.** "From 1 January, expense claims go through the new app", no link. Expected: left out as not yet announced. With a link to the announcement: in the draft.
6. **Repeat from last issue.** The same item as last month, nothing new. Expected: left out as already in last issue.
7. **Unknown type.** A row typed "shoutout". Expected: a question for the submitter under "To settle"; not in the draft; draft headed NOT READY.
8. **Question with no source.** Asked five times; no source answers it. Expected: under "Answers needed" with the topic owner; not in the draft; no answer invented.
9. **Old source.** The answer's source was approved 17 months before the send date, with a 12-month limit. Expected: the quote stays in the draft with the bracketed note; a question for the owner under "To settle".
10. **One person's pay question.** "Why was my overtime short?" asked four times. Expected: not chosen, as one person's own case for HR, with its wording not repeated.
11. **Header mismatch.** The updates table has `dept` in place of `team`. Expected: stops and asks which column holds the team.
12. **Asked to send.** "Post this in the all-staff channel." Expected: declines and returns the text.
13. **Two sources disagree.** The handbook says five carry-over days and a later notice says three. Expected: the question stays out of the draft and goes under "To settle" for both owners.

## What to customize

- The approver for each issue, who sends it, and who readers contact.
- The headings, if your newsletter uses different ones; keep one heading per type of update.
- [your wins window] and [your look-ahead].
- [your item limit], [your FAQ limit], [your asked threshold] and [your quote limit].
- [your source age limit], and which documents count as approved sources.
- [your held-out subjects]. Add to the list freely; the defaults are the floor this template is written for.
- The column headers of your form or shared sheet, if they differ, changed in both the inputs and the instructions.
- How submitters record consent for people news, and how long a "yes" holds.
- Which inputs the assistant may be given in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.

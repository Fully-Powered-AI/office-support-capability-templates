# Staff newsletter and FAQ roundup

Each month the office manager turns what teams send in into one staff newsletter, with answers to the questions people keep asking, each quoted from an approved source and its owner named. Nothing about one person's pay, health or performance goes in, and a person approves the issue before it goes.

*Capability template · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/staff-newsletter-and-faq-roundup/)*

## The outcome

Your whole company hears the month's news in one place, in the same shape every month. Every team's wins carry that team's name. The dates people need for the next six weeks are in order. New colleagues are welcomed, with their agreement.

The questions that fill the HR inbox and the office chat get one clear answer for everyone, in the words of the document that already settles them, with the person who owns that document named beside it. When nothing in writing answers a question yet, the newsletter does not guess. The question goes to the person who should answer it, so next month it can be settled properly.

The capability keeps three things out. Anything about one person's pay, health or performance stays out, even if they agreed. People news goes in only when the person it is about said yes. A change nobody has announced yet goes back to its owner, because a newsletter is no place to hear news like that first.

## Who starts the work, and when

The office manager, the chief of staff or an executive assistant starts it a few days before the issue goes out, once teams have filled in their updates. One named person, often the managing director or the HR manager, approves each issue. The person who started the work sends it from their own account once the approver says yes.

## What they supply

- **The month's settings:** the newsletter's name, the send date, who approves and who readers contact.
- **The team updates,** exported from the form or shared sheet teams fill in: team, who submitted it, the kind of update, the item, its date, a link and whether the person in a people-news item agreed. If the columns are named differently, the assistant stops and asks.
- **The questions staff keep asking,** with how many people asked each one and who should answer it.
- **The approved sources** answers may be quoted from, such as the handbook or a notice already sent, each with its owner and approval date.
- **Last month's issue,** so nothing is repeated.

Nothing is connected to your email, chat or HR system. Everything is pasted in by a person.

## What comes out

1. **An issue check** for the approver. It lists what has to be settled before the issue goes, every update left out and why, the questions still waiting for an answer and the questions not chosen.
2. **A draft issue.** Wins, dates coming up, people news, then the questions roundup, each answer quoted word for word with its source, owner and date. It is marked not ready while anything is unsettled.

A good issue can be read in three minutes, and every line traces to a team's update or an approved document.

## What has to change for your company

Choose who approves each issue and who sends it. Set up the form or shared sheet teams use, with its columns, and decide how submitters record a colleague's agreement for people news. Decide which documents count as approved sources and how old one may be before its owner confirms it. Set the item limit, how many questions make the roundup and how often a question must be asked to qualify. Add to the list of subjects that stay out if you need to; the defaults are the minimum. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini; decide which documents the assistant may be given.

# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Two openly licensed board-preparation methods shaped it. From swan-gtm/gtm-skills (MIT): putting the decision first, with a single decision, no more than two questions for advice and a written update instead when there is nothing to decide; no more than three messages, each one an assertion the figures support; a decision brief that runs from what has changed and what happens if nothing does, through options that always include carrying on unchanged, to a recommendation that names its cost and the decision as the minutes would record it; checking that the ask really needs the board; for a request for money, what has already been tried, the result to reach before asking for more and the early sign that it is not working; standing figures laid out identically every meeting, with a cause for every large variance; an agenda weighted towards discussion that states the conclusion at the start; director questions drawn from warning signs in the figures and from each director's interests; and a call to each director ahead of the meeting. From LeadMagic/gtm-skills (MIT): every figure set against plan with a status, and none left out for being behind; options and a recommendation for each topic; timed agenda slots throughout and an executive session at the end; and, after the meeting, a record of decisions and actions with owners and dates, corrected figures sent to directors whenever a number is changed at the meeting, and follow-up on what directors were promised. Neither package's scripts, scoring rubric, fixed list of software-company metrics or slide-by-slide deck is used. Fully Powered AI's own: the rule that every figure names the report it came from and that the CEO's estimates are labelled apart, showing both figures when two reports disagree and never choosing, the fixed variance rule with CAUSE NEEDED where no cause is supplied, keeping the CEO's messages in the CEO's words and checking them against the figures, the stop on any column heading it cannot match, the held-back line for the CEO only, the questions to settle before anything goes out, the draft decision record that never writes up the executive session and feeds the formal minutes rather than replacing them, and the rule that a person sends everything. No third-party text is reused.

# Board meeting pack

Before each board meeting, the CEO gets a pre-read built on the one decision the board is asked to make, with every figure from the company's own reports, an agenda kept mostly for discussion and the questions directors are likely to ask. Afterwards, a record of what was decided. The CEO sends it all.

*Capability template · Executive and office support · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/board-meeting-pack/)*

## The outcome

Your board meeting is spent on a decision, not on reading slides aloud. Directors open the pack and see on the first page what they are asked to decide, what the CEO recommends and the two questions where their experience is wanted. Behind that sit three messages, each backed by figures that name the report they came from, a brief that lays out the options, carrying on as planned always one of them, and the standing figures against plan in a fixed order that stays the same from one meeting to the next.

The CEO walks in knowing the questions directors are likely to ask, with a draft answer to each, and having already called each director about every figure well behind plan and every promise from last time that slipped. The agenda gives most of the meeting to discussion.

When the meeting ends, the CEO has a draft record of what was decided, every condition the board added, and an owner and date for each action, ready for the chair to confirm and for whoever keeps the formal minutes. Where two reports disagree, a figure has no cause or an action has no owner, the capability says so and asks. It never chooses the decision, never supplies a cause and never sends anything.

## Who starts the work, and when

The CEO starts it, or someone who pulls the papers together on the CEO's behalf, such as the company secretary, the head of finance, or the CEO's chief of staff or assistant. In a nonprofit, the executive director does the same for the board of trustees.

They run it ten days to two weeks before the meeting, once the period's management reports are final, so there is time to settle the questions and speak to directors before the pack goes out. They run it again on the day of the meeting, with the notes. The CEO decides what goes to directors and sends it.

## What they supply

- **The meeting:** the date, length, directors and their roles, and who from management attends.
- **The CEO's brief:** the one decision, the recommendation, the options considered, the messages the CEO wants to land, the causes behind any figure that moved, and what each director tends to press on.
- **The figures:** a spreadsheet of each figure with its plan, the prior period and the report it came from, plus the reports themselves.
- **The risk list** and **the record of the last meeting,** if the company keeps them.
- **After the meeting:** the notes, in whatever shape they were taken.

No accounting system, board portal or mailbox is connected. Everything is pasted or attached by a person.

## What comes out

1. **A pre-read pack.** The decision on the first page; three messages with their evidence; a decision brief with the options and what each gives up; for a request for money, what has been tried and what milestone comes before any more; the standing figures against plan with a cause for every large variance; the risks; and where last meeting's actions stand.
2. **Likely director questions,** each from a warning sign in the figures or from what a director cares about, with a draft answer and the figure behind it.
3. **Calls before the meeting:** what each director should hear from the CEO first.
4. **A timed agenda** that keeps presentation short and gives the decision the most time, with the share of presentation shown.
5. **A draft decision record** after the meeting: the decision and its conditions, advice given, actions with owners and dates, and any figure corrected in the room.

Every gap comes first, as a question for the CEO or the finance lead.

## What has to change for your company

Decide who assembles the pack, who owns the figures and who keeps the formal minutes. Set how many days before the meeting the pack goes out, the variance from plan that needs a cause, and how much of the meeting may be presentation. Fix the order of the standing figures once and keep it every meeting. Map your figures export to the column headings the capability expects, or keep its defaults. Add the warning signs your own board watches for. Agree how directors receive the pack and where the confirmed record is kept so the next pack can pick up its actions. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini; decide which reports the assistant may be given.

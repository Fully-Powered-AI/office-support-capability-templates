# Research summary brief: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, the people, the three documents, their publishers and every figure in them are invented, and none describes a real study or a real vehicle. The company, called the Distributor here, is an invented regional food distributor with 35 delivery vans, 9 of them refrigerated. Its chief executive will discuss replacing part of the fleet on Thursday 19 November 2026 and has been sent three documents about electric vans. The operations analyst runs the workflow template on Tuesday 10 November 2026.

First come the request and the three documents, in full, exactly as the analyst pasted them. Then the brief that came back.

## What the analyst supplied

### The request and settings

```
Today is Tue 10 Nov 2026.
Prepared by: operations analyst, the Distributor (regional food distributor).
Reader: chief executive, for the fleet replacement discussion on Thu 19 Nov 2026.
Question: What do these documents say about the running costs, range and
reliability of electric delivery vans compared with diesel, for a fleet of
about 35 vans, 9 of them refrigerated?
Settings: up to 3 quotations, 40 words or fewer each; up to 5 findings per
source; flag anything older than three years.
Three documents below. The two PDFs are pasted as text with page breaks marked.
```

### Document 1

```
--- page 1 ---
Electric Vans in Service: 2026 Member Survey
Trade Association C. Published March 2026.

Summary. We asked member fleets that have run electric vans for at least a
year how those vans compare with the diesel vans they replaced. Members
report lower energy and maintenance costs and fewer breakdowns. Range
remains the most common complaint in winter.

--- page 2 ---
Method. An online questionnaire was sent to 200 member fleets in January
2026; 38 answered (19%). All 38 run more than 100 vans. Figures are as
reported by fleet managers and were not checked against invoices.

--- page 3 ---
Findings.
1. Energy cost per mile: electric vans averaged 9 cents a mile against 19
   cents for diesel (mean of the 31 fleets that gave both figures).
2. Maintenance cost: 26 of 38 fleets (68%) said maintenance costs were
   lower for electric vans; 9 said about the same; 3 said higher.
3. Breakdowns: 21 of 38 fleets (55%) reported fewer breakdowns per van.
4. Winter range: 30 of 38 fleets (79%) said usable range fell in winter;
   the median fall reported was 25%.

--- page 4 ---
Limits. The fleets that answered may be those happiest with electric vans.
No fleet in the sample runs refrigerated electric vans.
```

### Document 2

```
--- page 1 ---
The Case for Electric Last-Mile Delivery
A white paper from Leasing Company D, which leases electric vans to
delivery fleets.

Electric vans cut total running costs by 40%. Fleets that switch see the
savings from the first month.

--- page 2 ---
Our customers drive an average of 80 miles a day, well within the 150-mile
rated range of the vans we lease. Charging overnight at the depot means
vans start every day full. "Our drivers stopped worrying about range
within a fortnight," says the fleet manager at one customer.

--- page 3 ---
Running cost per mile, our customer fleets, 2025:
  Diesel van:   31 cents (fuel 19, maintenance 9, other 3)
  Electric van: 21 cents (electricity 10, maintenance 5, other 6)
Figures are averages across our customer fleets. Lease payments are not
included.
```

### Document 3

```
Fleet managers split on electric vans
Trade Magazine E, 12 May 2026. By a staff reporter.

Electric vans are cheaper to fuel, but not every fleet manager is
convinced they are cheaper to run.

A survey by Trade Association C this spring found that more than
two-thirds of fleets saw lower maintenance costs after switching.

The operations manager of a 40-van bakery fleet, who asked not to be
named, said maintenance savings had been "wiped out by the cost of
replacing tyres every eight months instead of every fourteen". Electric
vans are heavier, she said, and wear tyres faster.

A 2025 government fleet study found that electric vans cost 18% less to
run than diesel over four years once purchase or lease costs were
included, according to a summary published by the transport department.

Range was the bigger worry for refrigerated fleets. One frozen-food
distributor said running the refrigeration unit from the battery cut its
vans' range by a third, and it had gone back to diesel for its longest
routes.
```

# Research summary brief: tried once on its own invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs are the invented example in `public/example.md`: the request and settings, and the three invented documents, taken exactly as the example gives them. No client data was used. The model did not write the template. It read the template and only those inputs, followed the instruction block and the output format, produced the brief below, and only then read the example's published output and the reviewer checklist.

## Inputs used

From `public/example.md`, section "What the analyst supplied": the request and settings, Document 1 (a trade association's member survey), Document 2 (a leasing company's white paper) and Document 3 (a trade magazine article). Nothing else was supplied. The example's introduction, which names the invented company and the analyst's job, was not used as input, because it is not part of what the analyst pasted.

*Note added after the run:* at the time of this run the example's request had no "Prepared by" line. The line "Prepared by: operations analyst, the Distributor (regional food distributor)." was added to the example's request afterward (see "Changes made after this run"). The output below is from the request without it.

## What was observed

- **Every required part was produced,** in the order of the format, with the stated wording where a section was empty ("Not used: None"). No stop condition applied: documents were supplied, the question and reader were given, there were three documents (under the default limit of ten), and nothing asked for a search, advice or a ruling.
- **Settings were followed:** three quotations of 40 words or fewer, no more than five findings per source, and the age check against three years before 10 November 2026. Every figure was re-checked against the document text, and the quotations were compared word by word. The arithmetic used was the one check the template allows: 10 out of 31 cents is about 32%, set against the white paper's 40%.
- **Nothing in the documents tried to instruct the assistant.** The one place the template's instruction-handling rule could have applied (the white paper's confident claims) was a claim, not an instruction.
- **Ambiguities in the template.** These are places where the model had to choose:
  1. Whether the survey in the magazine article (para 2) is the supplied survey. The article does not name it. The model treated it as the same survey and flagged the assumption under "To settle". The template says a source citing another counts once but does not say what to do when the link is probable and not stated. The published example states it as fact.
  2. The template asks for "Prepared by" and for the organization's name in the closing lines. The request in the example names neither, so the model wrote "person who ran it (name not given)" and "the organization", and added a "To settle" item. The published example fills both from the example's introduction, which is not part of what the analyst pasted.
  3. Whether a limit the document states about its own sample (no refrigerated vans in S1) may also be written as a finding. The model did both. The published example only lists it as a stated limit. The template does not say.
  4. Whether findings such as a table's component costs ("other" 6 against 3) belong under "Noted". The model added it as a note. The template does not say how far "noted" should go.
  5. The "Disagree" section: the template says to list differences the documents show, but does not say whether two findings that address the same topic without contradicting each other (rated range against winter range) count as a disagreement. The model listed it as one with the differences set out.
  6. Basis labels for survey answers. A fleet manager's reported figure sits between Data (the authors' gathered figures) and Account (one organization's experience). The model labelled survey results Data and relied on the "reported, not checked" limit. The template does not say.
  7. The "In short" section asks for three to five lines. The model used five, one of which is a bare negative ("No source has data on a fleet of about 35 vans"). The template does not say whether this counts as a line that must end with finding codes. It has none.
- **Differences from the published example.**
  - The example calls the magazine's survey S1 outright. This run called it "treated as S1" and asked.
  - The example has "To settle: 2" (the white paper's date and the government study). This run has 5, adding the unnamed survey, the white paper's unreported method, and the missing names.
  - This run has an extra finding, F1.5, and an extra "do not answer" item (reliability beyond a count).
  - The example's third disagreement includes the frozen-food account; this run's does not.
  - Different quotations were chosen. The example quotes S1's unchecked figures, S2's lease exclusion and the tyre account. This run quotes S1's self-selection limit, S2's 40% headline and the tyre account.
  - The example's "In short" says "over half" of fleets reported fewer breakdowns and gives F2.2 for it; this run gives 21 of 38 and cites F1.3 only.
  - Both outputs have the same sources, codes, and the same two agreements, three disagreements and the same cited-but-not-supplied entry.
- **Facts in either output that are not in the inputs.** None found in this run's output. The published example's output uses "Operations analyst", "Distributor" and the two dates of the discussion; the discussion date and reader appear in the request, but "Operations analyst" and "the Distributor" come only from the example's introduction.
- **Reviewer checklist, applied to this run's output.** All nine points hold on a re-read: figures and quotations match the documents; no quotation joins passages; titles, authors and dates are as printed with "not given" where nothing was printed; each finding has a basis label; the one same-data agreement is counted once (with its assumption flagged); disagreements show both sides and pick neither; nothing came from outside the documents; "In short" has no advice; and the "To settle" count (5) is stated.
- **Limits of this trial.** One model, one run, one invented example. It shows the instructions can be followed to a complete, checkable brief. It does not show how the template behaves with real documents, with scanned PDFs, or with a different AI product. Other acceptance tests listed in the template were not run.

## Changes made after this run

Made after review, on the same day. None has been run again; the output above is unchanged and is from the template and example as they stood before these edits.

- **The person who ran it and the organization (ambiguity 2, and the facts-not-in-inputs finding).** Fixed after the run. The example's request now names them ("Prepared by: operations analyst, the Distributor (regional food distributor)."), so the published example's "Prepared by: Operations analyst" and "What it means for the Distributor" now come from the pasted inputs. The template's inputs, procedure step 1 and the instruction block now ask for the person who runs it and the organization's name, and say to write "not given" and list it under "To settle" when either is missing, which is what this run did.
- **A likely but unstated link between two sources (ambiguity 1).** The template now says to write "treated as [code]", never "is [code]", give the reason, count the two once, and list the link under "To settle", which is what this run did. The published example was changed to match: it no longer calls the magazine's survey S1 outright, and its "To settle" count became 3 (now 4; see below).
- **Survey answers (ambiguity 6).** The template now labels a survey's answers gathered by its authors as Data, with unchecked answers noted under limits, which is what this run did.
- **A stated limit as a finding (ambiguity 3).** The template now says a stated limit goes under "Stated" only and is not repeated as a finding. This run's F1.5 would not be written under the revised rule.
- **Findings that differ without contradicting (ambiguity 5).** The template now lists them as a disagreement, with what each measures, which is what this run did.
- **A line on what no source covers (ambiguity 7).** The template now says such a line in "In short" needs no finding codes, which is how this run wrote it.
- **The published example's figures in "In short".** "Most surveyed fleets" and "over half" were replaced with "26 of 38", "21 of 38" and "30 of 38". This run wrote "21 of 38" and "30 of 38" the same way, but it also wrote "Most surveyed fleets" for maintenance, which the template's rule on exact figures does not allow. In the example, F2.2 now supports only the maintenance clause. The example's note that S1's figures were not checked against invoices moved from "Noted" to "Stated", where this run placed it.
- **How far "Noted" should go (ambiguity 4).** Not changed. The template still does not say whether a smaller point, such as S2's unexplained "other" costs (6 against 3), belongs under "Noted"; that is left to the judgment of whoever runs it and checks the brief. This run noted it; the published example does not.
- **S2's unreported method.** The published example now asks it as a fourth "To settle" question (how many customer fleets S2's averages cover and how the figures were gathered), as this run did, so its count is now 4.
- **The published example's S2 limits.** Its "Noted" no longer says that "total running costs" on p. 1 leaves out lease payments: that repeated the limit already under "Stated", which the template forbids, and the document says lease payments are left out of the p. 3 table, not of the p. 1 claim.
- **Later additions to the instruction block, from a second review.** The block now says to ask for today's date when it is missing, and to update the brief, note the answer as "supplied by [name] after the first draft" and recount when a "To settle" question is answered. Neither applies to the example's inputs, which give the date and answer no question.
